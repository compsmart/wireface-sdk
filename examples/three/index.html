Wireface SDK: three.js examples

One small page per idea: each shows a few lines of face code inside an ordinary three.js scene. View the source of any page; stage.js is the shared three.js set-up (renderer, lights, orbit controls), and hello.html does it all by hand. The whole cast is on one stage in the demo.

Getting started

  • HelloThe smallest page: a body, a head group, and a FaceMesh with Sarah's skin that blinks, looks at you and talks.
  • Build your own characterA snowman from primitives: face.bounds places the hat; face.skinMaterial() makes the snow match the face.
  • Hosted character riggingCreate a prepared 2D picture or 3D portrait, export it, then play it offline.

What faces do

  • ExpressionsEvery mood, standing moods, states, and your own expressions from the rig's channels.
  • Looking at thingsgazeAt() anything in the world: an orb, your pointer, the camera, each other. Eyes first, then the head.
  • StylesPhoto, sculpt, toon and hologram on the same lights; colour presets, the wireframe-to-skin cross-fade, sweeps and vanishing.
  • Two characters talkingTurn-taking with recorded, lip-synced lines: the listener watches the speaker and reacts.
  • Lip sync from a microphonelistenTo(): a robot that says what you say, live.
  • Talk with a characterA Gemini Live conversation through Wireface 2D's Conversation, on a three.js character. Needs a key on the dev server.

Faces as textures (FaceScreen)

  • A face on a screenWireface core's own renderer (the hologram with its bloom, or a skin) on a monitor's curved screen.
  • A talking portraitWireface 2D makes a painting talk, in a gilt frame on the wall, and its eyes follow you. Thirteen art styles.

Scale

  • A crowdTwo dozen FaceMesh heads (and more on a click) in one renderer, with a frame rate.
